# DECISIONS.md

One row per decision: what was chosen, its basis, and what would change it. A basis marked
"Measured" was measured on the simulator or on the public Opentrons protocol library at the
pinned commit (D-05); `provette corpus run` reruns the library-wide measurements.

## The trace and the state

| # | decision | basis | what would change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-09 | The trace comes from a tap on the simulator's command broker, in process. `opentrons.cli analyze --json-output` is a second, independent source, and the two must agree. | Measured: the broker gives op, well, volume, tip height, source line, parent call and an estimated time in one 1.3 s pass. Analyze gives liquids and module commands in an official schema but no line numbers. Both flagged the identical wells on the reference protocols. | The broker being removed; it is marked legacy, which is why the version is pinned (D-04) |
| D-10 | Below API 2.14 the simulator builds its analysis from the same broker messages we tap, so there the cross-check tests our reading of the messages, not the messages. From 2.14 the two sources are independent. | Measured on the tracer's fixtures across API 2.9 to 2.27 (`provette/trace/README.md`). Most of the library is below 2.14. | — |
| D-11 | An action by an 8-channel pipette is expanded by the tracer to the wells every channel enters, from the labware's own well positions. | Tested on 96- and 384-well plates, reservoirs, tip racks and tube racks, on both simulator engines and against both trace sources. A rack column that a single-channel pipette has already taken tips from gives fewer targets; the simulator itself raises no error there. | — |
| D-12 | Whether a tip touched liquid is modelled from liquid height and tip height, and an unknown volume downgrades the finding. | Measured: a naive "tip touched two wells" scan flagged 117 of 150 library protocols. Air gaps taken above a well and dispenses from above the liquid were most of it. | — |
| D-13 | When a protocol aspirates liquid with the tip above the liquid's known height, the pipette draws air: the contact verdict is `no_contact`, the tip gains air, nothing leaves the well, and the anomaly `aspirate_above_liquid` is recorded. The height is known where the trace carries the well's volume-to-height table (from the labware definition's `innerLabwareGeometry`) or the definition says the bottom is flat. Only where the geometry is unknown does the old reading stand: the liquid still moves, the verdict is `unknown`, and everything downstream is marked uncertain. | The column height is a floor: 20 µl in a 1.5 ml conical tube computes to 0.34 mm as a column, but the definition's cone puts it at 2.25 mm, above the default 1 mm aspirate height. Treating the column as the surface would silently erase every downstream finding; with the table the surface is the definition's, and 2 ml in a NEST 195 ml trough really is below a tip at 1 mm once 46 draws of 18 µl have left it (`SOURCE-TOO-SHALLOW`). | A well whose table puts the surface wrongly by more than the 0.1 mm contact tolerance, measured against the plastic |
| D-14 | The trace takes a well's shape from the newest version of the same standard labware definition when the version the protocol loads has none; the well's depth and maximum volume stay those of the loaded version. | At API 2.19 (the level the agent writes at) the simulator loads version 2 of the NEST 195 ml reservoir and version 1 of the 12-channel one; their shapes were added in versions 4 and 3. Only 26 of the 137 standard labware carry the shape in the version 2.19 loads; 73 do in their newest. The newer versions are Opentrons' remeasurements of the same plastic: the outlines differ by tenths of a millimetre (the 195 ml trough's depth 25 to 26.85 mm is the largest). Custom labware keeps only what its own file says. | A standard definition whose newest version describes different plastic from the loaded one |
| D-15 | A well nothing was declared about, and which the protocol only ever dispenses into, starts empty. | Most library protocols declare nothing (D-17). This under-reports contact where a plate really arrives holding sample; the manifest is where that knowledge will come from. | The manifest inferring starting contents |
| D-16 | In a protocol that declares no liquids, a well that is asked for more than the robot ever put into it was holding something: it is an origin from the start of the run, with its own identity and an unknown volume. In a protocol that does declare liquids, other wells start empty and the shortfall stays an anomaly. | Measured on 40 real traces: over-draws and draws from "empty" wells were plates and tubes that arrive holding undeclared liquid. Without this, cross-contamination between the samples of an undeclared plate is invisible. Known cost: where nothing is declared, over-aspirating a well the robot filled itself creates a phantom origin, and dispenses into such plates become volume-unknown. | The manifest supplying starting contents |

## The rules

| # | decision | basis | what would change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-17 | Rules come in two tiers, structural and role-based, and a finding says which it is and whether its roles were declared or inferred. | Measured: 11 of 833 library protocols declare liquids. The rest describe them in README prose and deck images. | — |
| D-18 | Findings carry a severity: error, warning or note. Where sources disagree the rule is a warning and quotes both. | Integra says a tip may be reused down a serial dilution if blown out after each mix; Svec et al. used a fresh tip each step. | — |
| D-19 | When liquids are undeclared and one line sends one tip into several stocks, the contamination finding is a note, not an error. | Three of four flagged library protocols read were one reagent split over several reservoir channels and drawn with one tip. Structural rules cannot tell that from eight patient samples; the role-based tier can, and raises it. | The manifest saying what the stocks hold |
| D-20 | Carry-over severity follows what was carried into what: a carrier (water, PBS, medium) is a note; a sample keeps the structural severity; a reagent into a sample or reagent is at most a warning; an unplaced name changes nothing. Roles come from a word list over the declared name that we own (`provette/rules/roles.py`, table in `docs/rule-sources.md`). | About half the errors on correct plates were a diluent tip entering the stock it was about to dilute, which is routine at the bench; the loop spent rounds repairing them. A note says it without blocking; a sample carried anywhere stays an error. | A finding a person judges harmful that the table calls a note |
| D-21 | WELL-CAPACITY's error fires only past the rim of the well, from the labware definition's shape; past the maker's stated maximum (`totalLiquidVolume`) but under the rim, or where no rim is known, it is a warning. | The stated maximum is a working volume, not the rim: by the definitions' own `innerLabwareGeometry` the NEST "100 µl" PCR well holds 217 µl to the rim, the "200 µl" flat well 368, the 1.5 ml tube 1,898. Over the public library (`docs/detection-catalogue.md`, WELL-CAPACITY flood test) 134 protocols cross a stated maximum; read by labware, 117 of the 123 that are not waste are under the rim or on custom definitions with nominal numbers, and 6 are real spills, all past the rim. An error that is wrong 117 times in 123 is not an error. | A vendor stating that liquid above `totalLiquidVolume` is unusable (a sealed PCR well under a heated lid may be); our triage finding the warnings ignored |
| D-22 | A rule may be written as data: a YAML file under `provette/rules/declared/`, one rule per file, in one of three shapes (`no_carry_over`: a tip that has been in a liquid of one kind must not enter a well of another; `full_well_not_mixed`: a well this full must not be mixed; `add_after`: one kind of liquid goes in after another), read in beside the Python rules when `provette.rules` is imported, every field checked so a broken file stops the import by name. A rule that fits none of the three shapes stays Python. | We write rules without writing code; the three shapes cover the failure classes the catalogue found that are about carry-over, fullness and order, which is what a biologist can state without code. The proof: ENZYME-BEFORE-DILUENT re-expressed as `declared/ENZYME-BEFORE-DILUENT.yaml` passes its fixtures with the same lines, wells and fingerprints it had as Python. A rule with its own arithmetic (a well's height, a magnet's settle time, a series' ratios) has no shape a form can offer without becoming a language, and a language is not what we asked for. | A fourth failure class we propose more than once that no shape fits; then a fourth shape, never a general language |
| D-23 | A declared rule with `status: proposed` runs on every check but every finding it makes is a note, titled "(proposed)", and can never block a submission; the product's rule form always writes `proposed`. Adopting a rule is changing `status` to `adopted` and writing `adopted_on` in the file, which is a commit on `main` and so passes our review like any change. | `provette check` exits 1 only on an error or a warning, and the agent's readiness and `verify` read that exit code, so a proposed rule can be watched on real drafts before anyone trusts it; a rule that floods is worse than no rule (PIPETTE-RANGE's low tier), and a first rule must be allowed to flood harmlessly. `api/declared.py` sets the status on save; the file is the only place adoption happens. | A proposed rule that has run clean on the library and the drafts for a week with nobody adopting it: then the form may offer adoption to a person who can commit |
| D-24 | The liquid word lists are data, `provette/rules/roles.yaml`, owned by us and read once at import (the product reloads after a word is added); a declared liquid that no list places is reported as *unplaced* in the check and verify output, and the product asks the person what it is beside the verdict and writes the answer into the file. Silence about an unknown liquid is never an answer. | D-20's severity table depends on the roles, and a name the lists cannot place left the finding at its structural severity without saying why; model-written drafts name liquids the lists miss (`stock_0`, `glycerol_A1`, kit names) and the catalogue's role-tier rules (ENZYME-BEFORE-DILUENT, NO-NEGATIVE-CONTROL) are silent on an unplaced name. A word list we extend from the names we meet is the plan's own mechanism for that, and a question beside the verdict is how the list grows without writing code. | A name asked about that a person cannot answer either (a kit's code name): then the request or the plate map must say what it is |
| D-25 | Beside every verdict the product shows what was checked for the protocol's technique and what was not, from `provette/rules/coverage.yaml` (one entry per technique word of the request sets and a default; `checked`, `not_checked` with the catalogue's row numbers, `physics`). A verified protocol was checked for the things listed and for nothing else, and the page says so: silence is not safety. | The catalogue's map has 33 rows and twelve are checks; a "verified" with no list beside it reads as "nothing is wrong", which is false for a bead cleanup (settle time, washes, drying unchecked) or an ELISA (washes, incubations, drying unchecked). A "verified" with nothing beside it can hide a protocol that would fail as an experiment in a way no rule checks yet; the table is the honest form of a verdict while the rules are fewer than the failures. | Every `not_checked` line of a technique becoming a rule, at which point the entry says so |
| D-26 | PyLabRobot's deleted contamination tracker is cited in the README as the nearest prior art. | It existed from June 2024 to December 2025: a per-tip and per-well history set that raised an error on aspirate. It had no downstream propagation, no other rule family, and did not read Opentrons simulator traces as far as we could verify. | Finding something closer |

## The plate map and the agent

| # | decision | basis | what would change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-27 | A plate map in the product pins every well. Position-free groups exist only for checking protocols written before there was a map. | A map that leaves positions free cannot tell which sample is in which well: the planted-bug run's only misses are samples swapped between wells (`eval/checks/README.md`). A map that pins positions catches the same swap as a mismatch. | — |
| D-28 | The product is the feedback loop for protocols written by small open models: plate map in; the model writes; the simulator, the plate-map check, the contamination rules, sample accounting and sufficiency verify; the loop repairs; a prep sheet comes out. | People who run liquid handlers say the missing thing is a feedback loop (`docs/pain-points.md`). A small model's first draft rarely verifies, and with the checks as its tools it gets there (`eval/agent/README.md`). The checks raise no false alarm on correct protocols and catch what the simulator passes (`eval/checks/README.md`). | A small model whose first drafts verify as often as the loop's final ones; then the loop is a convenience |
| D-29 | The plate-map planner (`provette plan`) may repair exactly one missing `]` in the model's JSON, reports that it did, and refuses anything else; its `same_as_source` reading of an unpinned plate-to-plate transfer stands as a stricter reading, not an error; the hand-written maps are not widened to match it. | Nemotron's JSON often ends one bracket short and the content is otherwise whole (the count is in `eval/planner/README.md`); a wrong map still fails `verify` against a correct protocol, which is what the check is for. | A repaired map that verifies a wrong protocol |
| D-30 | The claim "with Provette in the loop, an open Nemotron model writes protocols that verify, which told the rules alone it does not" rests on the loop, not on the model knowing the rules, and the pages say so with the baseline beside the result: the twelve rules written into the prompt as instructions, with no checker, against the same model with the checks in its loop. The baseline is quoted as evidence for the loop, never as a page about the model's mistakes. | `eval/baseline/README.md` beside `eval/agent/README.md`: told the rules, the model still breaks them; what moves the number is a report it can act on. | A model that, told the rules, verifies as often as with the loop: then the loop is a convenience, and the pages must say that instead |
| D-31 | The pages never say "no engineer needed". What they say: for the techniques the checks cover, a biologist who does not program works on their own; for a new procedure they are told what was not checked and can teach the checker without code (what a liquid is, and rules through the form); a run the loop cannot repair needs one person per lab who reads code, who gets a two-line note of where it stopped rather than a debugging session. | The runs the loop does not close are mostly plate-map layouts and prep-sheet flags on Super and reasoning that runs out of tokens on Nano; a few leave a rule finding standing when the budget ends (`eval/agent/README.md`, every failure read). A tool that claimed more would be trusted once. | The share of stuck runs, measured across both request sets, being small enough that the sentence can give the number instead |
| D-32 | A submit the checks still reject is refused until the model has written three drafts; the refusal tells it what to read and to write the whole file again. The evaluation runs with the same setting as the product (`api/run_settings.py`), so its numbers describe what a person gets. | A model that submits its first failing draft ends not verified on requests it can finish in four drafts; the prompt already asked it to try again, and nothing enforced it. | A measured run showing the refusal costs more than it verifies |
| D-33 | A request passes two checks before a map is proposed. First a word gate before any model call (`provette/planner/gate.py`): only text with no lab word and no amount is refused, and the page offers Send anyway. Then the planner may call `not_a_request` with one sentence ("plan it anyway" plans without it). A file for Check a protocol is refused only when it has no `def run(` (or is binary): a protocol with a syntax error still goes to the simulator, which names the line. | Without it, "What is your name and what is the purpose of life?" was planned: the model's reasoning said there was no lab request, but its only way to end was `propose_platemap`, so it invented a map of one well of water. Every request on file and 23 bench phrasings pass the gate, 8 off-topic messages do not (`tests/planner/test_gate.py`); 833 of 833 library protocols pass the file check (`tests/api/test_checks.py`); the planner's run on the 17 requests says how often it declines a real one (`eval/planner/README.md`). | A real request refused by the gate (then its word joins `_LAB_WORDS`, with a test); the planner declining a real request (then the prompt's paragraph is narrowed, or the tool dropped) |

## Where it runs

| # | decision | basis | what would change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-34 | The sandbox runs a pre-built image with `opentrons` inside; a local Docker runner sits behind the same interface. | Documentation: sandboxes are beta, import OCI images, have networking on by default and a 12 GiB layer cap. Limits and stability through December are not promised (D-35). | — |
| D-35 | Token Factory Sandboxes are used for the checks when `--runner sandbox` is given; the local runner stays the default for development. Measured once beta access was granted: image ready in 0.7 s, one check 14.4 s round trip, five at once in 11.6 s; the service states 3600 s per run, 50 concurrent, 12 GiB; CPU, memory and the `cost` unit (0.0956 per check) are undocumented. | `provette/sandbox/README.md`; the runner passed its live tests unchanged once the grant existed. | A documented price that makes a check cost more than it is worth |
| D-36 | Where the demo is hosted: Fly.io, one always-on `shared-cpu-1x` Machine with 1 GB and 512 MB of swap in `ams`, one 1 GB volume, `provette.fly.dev` with Fly's TLS, $6.07 a month on our card; the deployment is `fly.toml` plus `deploy/fly-up.sh`, idempotent, from the same Dockerfile that builds the image here. | `docs/deploy.md` (seven providers priced from their own pages; Hetzner the fallback). Sized from measurements in the container (`deploy/README.md`): 62 MB idle, 208 MB peak for the largest episode's analysis, 272 MB for two at once; the worst plausible moment (two analyses beside two live runs) is about 630 MB. | Fly refusing the card (then the Hetzner steps in `docs/deploy.md`); hackathon credits covering Nebius AI Cloud compute (same Dockerfile, a VM instead); the memory graph reaching 1 GB (then `memory = "2gb"`, $11.11). **Changed for the judging (2026-09-28): `performance-2x`, two dedicated CPUs and 4 GB.** Measured on the live Machine: a shared CPU is throttled under sustained checks (the CPU's steal time was about seven times its own work), a draft's checks went from 3–24 s to 178–237 s and then timed out, and a timeout read as a rejection. The size is `fly.toml`'s; `deploy/fly-size.sh` changes it in place. **Changed 2026-10-02: the Machine stops when idle** (`auto_stop_machines = "stop"`, `min_machines_running = 0`). Always on at that size was about $2 a day with nobody using it; stopped it is about $0.25 a month plus about $0.09 for each hour it runs. Measured on the deployment: it stopped after about 7 idle minutes, stayed stopped, and the first request was answered in 4.4 s. Rejected: the shared CPU always on ($6.07, but the checks time out), a VM elsewhere (no cheaper, and ours to operate), a free tier that reclaims idle instances. What would change it: a day when nobody may wait four seconds (then `"off"` and `1` for that day), or live runs cut off because their page was closed |
| D-37 | What the deployment keeps on its volume, and where a live draft's checks run. The volume (`/data`) holds the live episodes, the checks, the analysis cache, the spend ledger, the failed-run records **and the three files a person edits from the pages** (`roles.yaml`, `sources.yaml`, `declared/*.yaml`), seeded from the image on the first boot and never overwritten after; `PROVETTE_ROLES_FILE`, `PROVETTE_SOURCES_FILE` and `PROVETTE_DECLARED_DIR` are read at import by the rules modules so every subprocess sees the same files as the API. The runner is `local` (subprocesses in the container); `nebius` is one environment variable away. | A taught word or a saved rule that vanished on the next deploy would make the "teach the checker without code" claim (D-31) false; measured in the container: a rule saved through the form fired in the next check and survived a restart. `local` because it is fastest (6 s a check against 14 s) and depends on nothing outside the Machine; the sandbox is a beta service and the demo must not stop when it does. | The sandbox's price being documented and the extra 8 s a check being worth the sentence on the episode page (then `PROVETTE_RUNNER = "nebius"` in `fly.toml`); a second Machine (then the in-memory rate limits and the process-wide simulation slots need a shared store) |
